## 2. Proven techniques

### 2.1 AI-generated text detection

- **Perplexity + burstiness (GPTZero origin):** sentence perplexity under a GPT-2-like LM; humans write high-variance perplexity ("bursty"), LLMs write with consistently low perplexity. GPTZero's old FAQ said perplexity > 85 → likely human; they **retired the statistical frontend in autumn 2023** for a learned model, but kept it as one of 7 "indicators" and stress it remains a cheap, explainable, first-principles detector used by many products (ZeroGPT, Copyleaks, Originality, Writer.ai). Sources: [GPTZero statistical approach](https://gptzero.me/news/statistical-approach), [EMNLP 2023 CT(2) paper which formalizes perplexity/burstiness/NLC families](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.136.pdf).
- **OpenAI's own classifier retired:** 26% true-positive rate on AI text, 9% false-positive rate on human text; pulled July 20 2023 for "low rate of accuracy"; unreliable on code; performance got worse on newer models. Sources: [OpenAI blog](https://openai.com/index/new-ai-classifier-for-indicating-ai-written-text/), [ArsTechnica](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2023/07/openai-discontinues-its-ai-writing-detector-due-to-low-rate-of-accuracy/).
- **Zero-shot / statistical methods (white-box, no training):**
  - *DetectGPT* (Mitchell, ICML 2023): machine text sits in negative-curvature regions of an LM's log-probability; requires perturbing text (expensive) — 0.81 → 0.95 AUROC over prior zero-shot. [arXiv 2301.11305](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11305)
  - *Fast-DetectGPT* (ICLR 2024): same idea but via **conditional probability curvature** and sampling instead of perturbing — ~2 orders of magnitude faster, machine-text curvature ≈ 3 vs human ≈ 0. [ICLR paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/6b8c6f846c3575e1d1ad496abea28826-Paper-Conference.pdf)
  - *Binoculars* (Allen AI, arXiv 2401.12070): ratio of perplexities of two LMs (observer + performer), zero-shot, no training; strong in a 2025 practical-comparison of zero-shot detectors ([NAACL-Findings review](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.271.pdf)).
- **Fine-tuned open detectors on Hugging Face (verified IDs, see Verified Facts below):** RoBERTa-base-OpenAI-detector (GPT-2 era, ~95% on GPT-2 outputs only — near-useless vs 2026 LLMs; modern_arrays ROC-AUC ≈ 0.31 in a third-party benchmark); `Hello-SimpleAI/chatgpt-detector-roberta` (HC3-trained RoBERTa); `desklib/ai-text-detector-v1.01` (DeBERTa-v3-large, RAID-leaderboard leader at submission; 0.875 ROC-AUC, 83.9% recall on raw AI text at a matched 0.5% FPR); `wasitaigeneratedcom/ai-text-detector-small` ("tropa-mini", 0.968 ROC-AUC). The benchmark data with matched FPRs: [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/wasitaigeneratedcom/ai-text-detector-small).
- **Known false-positive regimes (critical for us — Indian student text is heavily ESL):**
  - Stanford Liang et al., *Patterns* 2023: 7 popular detectors, 91 TOEFL essays — **61.3% average false-positive rate for non-native writers** (vs ~0% for native US 8th-graders); 97% of TOEFL essays flagged by ≥1 detector, 19% flagged unanimously. Prompting ChatGPT to "add literary language" (raising perplexity) crashed detection to near zero. [arXiv 2304.02819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02819), [Stanford HAI explainer](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-detectors-biased-against-non-native-english-writers).
  - EMNLP 2026 industry track: professional editing of non-native manuscripts "raises concern" — polished style, not AI authorship, drives many positives ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-industry.109.pdf)).
  - ETS' large-scale GRE study found feature-based (linguistic feature) detectors near-perfect and *not* biased; perplexity-based detectors were the biased class ([Computers & Education 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360131524000848)).
- **What evidence says:** No AI detector is reliable *as a sole verdict*. Perplexity/burstiness is cheap, explainable and locally implementable (GPT-2-small full perplexity on CPU is fine) but biased toward low-diversity (ESL) text; fine-tuned classifiers are stronger but still non-native-biased; zero-shot statistical methods (Fast-DetectGPT/Binoculars) are the more principled local option but need a small LM (GPT-J, ~6 GB — heavier). **Design: use a small classifier + perplexity/burstiness ensemble as a *secondary* signal with conservative thresholds, length gating, and explicit "uncertain" middle band (mirroring Turnitin's 1–19% withheld band and GPTZero's 40–70% band).**

### 2.2 Cross-modal / spoken-content detection (the MP4-vs-PDF scenario)

- **ETS = the only mature research line on "plagiarized spoken responses"** (students memorize canned answers from web sources and recite them in speaking tests):
  - Evanini & Wang (W14-1803, 2014): transcribe speech (ASR) → text-to-text similarity classifier (n-gram overlap/BLEU, LDA, WMD-style embedding features) → **92.0% accuracy from human transcripts, 87.1% from ASR output** (baseline 50%). Only a **5–10% accuracy drop from ASR errors** → approach is robust to transcription noise. [ACL paper](https://aclanthology.org/W14-1803.pdf); extended ETS work [W19-4445](https://preview.aclanthology.org/stats/W19-4445.pdf), patent [US11417339B1](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11417339).
  - **AutoSSD (ETS, 2026):** productionized "Automated Speech Similarity Detector" — ASR → text similarity → pair flagged for human review; explicitly notes transcription noise *reduces* cross-speaker similarity and that reviewers inspect the original audio too. Positioning: "similarity detection for written text is much more mature; underlying idea is to make it applicable to spoken responses by transcribing." ([ETS Research Memo RR-49](https://rr.ets.org/index.php/etsrr/article/download/49/40/81))
- **Practical implication (validated by ETS):** audio-shot *semantic+verbatim matching on the transcript* is the correct engineering approach; an identical-content-but-verbatim-spoken clip yields >0.9 cosine matches even with ASR noise. This is exactly Scenario 1 (MP4 reads another student's PDF paragraph).
- **Whisper-based transcript search:** transcript search products exist for podcasts (ListenNotes, Podscribe) and video (e.g., YouTube transcript indexes) — category confirms that transcript + text index is the canonical search stack; none of these do peer-vs-peer plagiarism; they are content search. We did not find *any* commercial product plugging Whisper transcripts into a plagiarism engine at scale — this appears to be a real gap our prototype fills.
- **Multimodal embedding research (CLIP/CLAP-style joint audio-visual-text spaces)** exists for retrieval, but is unnecessary here: the assignment's cross-modal path is *speech → text → text index*, i.e., an ASR front-end normalizes everything to one modality. Spending compute on audio embeddings is not justified for spoken-verbatim reuse. (Design conclusion, not a citation-forced one.)
- **Proctoring vendors (ProctorU, Examity, Honorlock):** public materials describe identity verification, browser locks, behavior monitoring, and AI-assisted proctoring — not content-similarity analysis of media. Unstop's own published guidance lists plagiarism/code-similarity detection and proctoring as *separate* controls ([Unstop blog](https://unstop.com/blog/how-candidates-cheat-in-online-assessments)). No public evidence of media-content plagiarism at these vendors.
- **What evidence says:** the proven recipe is *transcribe → align → same text-similarity machinery*, preserving per-segment timestamps from ASR (`segment.start/segment.end` in Whisper/faster-whisper), with metadata {file, t0, t1, confidence} attached to each chunk. Robustness to ASR noise means embeddings need not over-dominate scoring — semantic cosine over noisier transcript text still separates real reuse from noise.

### 2.3 Semantic + verbatim similarity mechanics

**Embedding model comparison (MTEB averages; all are CPU-friendly):**

| Model | Dim | Params | Max seq | MTEB avg (56 tasks) | Retrieval (15) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 | **384** | 22.7M | **256 tokens** | 56.26 | ~42 | Fastest (≈15 ms/chunk CPU); the baseline everyone uses; a 2025 PAN Foshan team explicitly found "all-MiniLM-L6-v2 was not robust enough, producing low recall" for plagiarism-aligned retrieval and chose **e5-base-v2** instead ([PAN'25 notebook](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4038/paper_324.pdf)). |
| intfloat/e5-small-v2 | 384 | 33.4M | 512 | 59.93 | 49.04 | Requires `query: `/`passage: ` prefixes; **HF card warns cosine scores distribute around 0.7–1.0** — absolute thresholds don't transfer across models ([card](https://huggingface.co/intfloat/e5-small-v2)). |
| BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 | 384 | 33.4M | 512 | 62.17 | 51.68 | ~1.5× slower than MiniLM, ~+20% retrieval quality; no prefix needed for passages ([card](https://huggingface.co/BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5)). |
| thenlper/gte-small | 384 | 33.4M | 512 | 61.36 | 49.46 | Similar class; no tokenizer tricks ([card](https://huggingface.co/thenlper/gte-small)). |
| FM: bge-base/e5-base (768-d) | 768 | 109M | 512 | 61.5–63.6 | 50–53 | ~3× slower; the Foshan PAN'25 winner's pick for *this exact plagiarism-retrieval task*. |

- **Task-specific calibration:** the mini model's cosine distribution between *unrelated* chunks is not flat — top-1-of-40-chunk in a 2026 MiniLM-vs-E5-vs-BGE test: MiniLM 1.0 P@1, E5 0.93, BGE 0.93 on a tiny in-house retrieval set ([measurement article](https://eworld.live/news/2026/09/embedding-model-pick-retrieval-precision)). Thresholds are model-specific; the assignment's ">0.80 cosine ⇒ paraphrase plagiarism" only holds for sentences close in mean length/context on MiniLM-class models — must be set *per model* with a calibration rule, not hardcoded blindly.
- **Hybrid sparse+dense + rerank is the strongest published configuration** for source retrieval in PAN 2026: BM25 + dense (BGE-M3) then a cross-encoder; "neither signal alone is sufficient", and the reranker salary is blended (β=0.7), not used as replacement ([PAN'26 ENGsTF](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4283/paper394.pdf)). PAN'26 Team JLP: BM25 + dense per sentence-span, RRF-fused, cross-encoder rerank → also best-in-class ([paper](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4283/paper400.pdf)). BioASQ 2026 independently confirms: hybrid > singles on recall, cross-encoder reranking moves Recall@K up from K=10 through K=300 ([notebook](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4283/paper49.pdf)).
- **Two-stage retrieval** (Foshan PAN'25 again): (1) doc-level global embedding → top-K candidate docs; (2) chunk-level FAISS ANN → reduce N² to KN; cache embeddings between stages. This matters directly for the 10,000-doc brief.
- **Chunking evidence:** "300–800 tokens for docs; not so large that BM25/dense dilute, not so small that context disappears; heading-aware with 10–20% overlap" ([production RAG playbook](https://optyxstack.com/rag-reliability/hybrid-search-reranking-playbook)); RAG frameworks commonly default to paragraph-boundary chunking near target size + 64-token overlap ([RAGBench doc](https://github.com/tamzid16/RAGBench/blob/main/docs/retrieval.md)). The assignment's 200–400-token + overlap prescription sits inside the safe band; for MiniLM-L6-v2 caps, 256-token context means ~200-token chunks are the practical ceiling (or the chunk tail gets truncated invisibly).
- **Cross-encoder rerankers:** `cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2` is the standard small reranker ([RAGBench doc](https://github.com/tamzid16/RAGBench/blob/main/docs/retrieval.md)) — scores (query, passage) jointly, cheap (~23M params).
- **Threshold evidence:** the assignment itself cites cosine > 0.80 for paraphrase; E5 warns 0.7–1.0 distributions ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/intfloat/e5-small-v2)); eWorld test: all-MiniLM-L6-v2 retrieval P@1 = 1.0 on its tiny set but degraded on harder paraphrase — set thresholds by *corpus-relative* calibration (e.g., chunk score > global chunk-pair mean + 3σ) rather than absolute cosine.
- **What evidence says:** embed with MiniLM-L6-v2 for speed (384-d, cheap ANN) but plan the flowing two-stage doc-level → chunk-level retrieval, sentence-aware chunking with 10–20% overlap, and explicit per-model threshold calibration; document the e5/BGE upgrade path as the Foshan team did, and note BM25 + cross-encoder as the published best-practice hybrid for source retrieval if precision is wanted.

### 2.4 Boilerplate / common-source discounting (Scenario 4)

- **Winnowing paper explicitly formalizes boilerplate discount:** "boilerplate is usually material supplied by a course instructor that is expected to be part of the final solution — i.e., it is sanctioned copying. Excluding boilerplate is easily done by fingerprinting the boilerplate with a special document ID that indicates any match with that fingerprint should be discarded." (Schleimer, Wilkerson, Aiken, SIGMOD 2003.) Concretely: fingerprint the shared case-prompt / instruction-citations set under a reserved ID; drop every match whose source ID is in the boilerplate set. Also gives noise threshold k: matches shorter than k tokens are suppressed entirely ([PDF](https://theory.stanford.edu/~aiken/publications/papers/sigmod03.pdf)).
- **MOSS "base file" mechanism** and **JPlag multi-base/support-code exclusion** are directly analogous — sanctioned shared code is excluded per file before comparison; industry-standard practice.
- **Empirical lesson from code plagiarism data (CONPLAG):** "some errors occurred due to matching of the I/O code, similar in form between solutions" — templates cause persistent false positives even in curated datasets ([arXiv 2303.10763](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.10763)). HackerEarth solves the analogous case explicitly: "correctly handling standard problems where similar solutions are expected" via logic-aware scoring rather than raw overlap ([HackerEarth](https://help.hackerearth.com/hc/en-us/articles/360003777793-Plagiarism-insights)).
- **Turnitin-style filters:** exclude quoted material and bibliography; exclude sources contributing <10% credit; DrillBit excludes <14 words per source automatically ([KVASU notice](https://www.kvasu.ac.in/files/1729320307_14_Plagiarism+detection+software+shift+DRILLBIT+EXTREME+Modification+to+the+prevailing+rules+related+to+plagiarism.pdf)). "Smallest unique matching" ideas in the literature align with Turnitin's approach: prefer *longest contiguous shared sequence* not found across ≥2 other documents over repeated short matches.
- **What evidence says:** the standard ways to implement Scenario 4 are (a) *before* peer matching, fingerprint the shared case-prompt text into a registry with special IDs and skip any chunk whose nearest neighbor is a registry chunk (winnowing's own recommendation), (b) discount matches that recur in ≥M documents (spread-out matches = institutional boilerplate; single-doc matches = plagiarism), (c) minimum match length (≈≤14 words discounted per DrillBit; our rule: matched chunks < ~100–150 chars don't advance the similarity score), (d) require ≥N independent non-prompt matched chunks before flagging a document HIGH.

### 2.5 Web-reference engines

- **Canonical mechanism** (from PAN shared-task systems and academic work): pick suspicious sentences/keyphrases → form search queries → send to engine → (a) compare snippets directly, or (b) download top-N pages and align full text. Specifics that worked in PAN systems: one query per 4-sentence chunk, ~40 queries per suspicious doc (Kong 2013); download-triggered when the snippet includes >20% of the query's word 2-grams (Suchomel & Brandejs, PAN'14); TF-IDF + noun-phrase queries ([Building the task](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-1180/CLEF2014wn-Pan-PotthastEt2014.pdf)). A 2012 paper formalizes "sentence → search query → snippet classifier (lexical + syntactic + LDA)"; a 2022 system uses POS-tag n-gram fingerprints for efficient candidate retrieval ([P12-3025](https://aclanthology.org/P12-3025.pdf), [Expert Sys w/ Apps 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417422001610)).
- **Free-tier API reality (verified 2026):**
  - DuckDuckGo "official" Instant Answer API is *not* a search API — returns only Wikipedia-style abstracts for well-known entities ([guide](https://link.sc/blog/duckduckgo-search-api-guide)). Everything else labeled "DDG API" (ddgs / duckduckgo-search package) is an unofficial scraper with real rate-limit risk — but is the backbone of multiple working open-source plagiarism checkers ([example](https://github.com/irgifebry/plagiarism-checker)).
  - **Brave Search API: free tier 2,000 queries/month**; SERP-API vendors ~500 requests/mo free; Google Programmable Search (custom search JSON API) official free tier is 100 queries/day (well-known constraint).
  - A 2026 ICOIN paper published the *exact architecture we planned*: Sentence-BERT chunk embeddings + DuckDuckGo no-key search + semantic cosine via local embeddings; no paid API ([ICOIN 2026](https://www.computer.org/csdl/proceedings-article/icoin/2026/11480560/2g3lDX139Be/abstract)).
- **Wikipedia / reference-KB approach:** preprocessing Wikipedia pages (or case-study source texts) into a local sentence index — this is Ouriginal's "published material" category but free/public; the assignment explicitly blesses "a set of Wikipedia articles or case study source texts" as the local KB.
- **What evidence says:** two-tier external engine is the correct prototype design: Tier 1 always-on = local KB (Wikipedia sections + case-prompt + LLM-generated corpus) with MiniLM similarity, fully offline/deterministic; Tier 2 optional = free search API (ddgs or Brave) with chunked distinctive-sentence queries, snippet display, rate-limit sleeps, and a **mock/fallback mode when network unavailable** (mirroring Copyleaks' `sandbox: true` mock pattern so the module is testable in CI without network).

### 2.6 Vector databases & scaling

- **FAISS guidelines** (official wiki + production guides): Flat = only exact; use when <10k vectors; IVF (IndexIVFFlat) needs training, `nlist` between 4√N and 16√N, tune `nprobe` at query time; HNSW = no training, fastest at high recall, ~`d*4 + M*8` bytes/vector, no vector deletion support; use IVF+PQ when memory-bound at ≥10M vectors ([official guidelines](https://github.com/facebookresearch/faiss/wiki/Guidelines-to-choose-an-index), [1M-vectors wiki](https://github.com/facebookresearch/faiss/wiki/Indexing-1M-vectors), [Pinecone's FAISS series](https://www.pinecone.io/learn/series/faiss/vector-indexes/)).
- **Cosine in FAISS:** there is no "cosine" metric; the idiom is `faiss.normalize_L2(vecs)` + **IndexFlatIP** — dot product over unit-norm vectors = cosine. Verified in [this tutorial code](https://buildwithaitoday.com/track/rag-knowledge/learn/rag-indexing_algorithms) and [reference doc](https://github.com/endman100/skill-router-nexus/blob/main/AI-Research/faiss/faiss/references/index_types.md).
- **Size math for our corpus:** 10,000 submissions × ~30 chunks each = 300k chunks × 384 dims × 4 bytes = **~460 MB of raw vectors** — trivially held in RAM; IndexFlatIP brute-force at 300k × 300k is ~9×10¹⁰ dot products/query — still fine for pairwise evaluation but IVFFlat/HNSW become the right answer beyond ~1M chunks, which corresponds to ~30–35k submissions (i.e., the 10k case is comfortably Flat, but the write-up should justify the IVF/HNSW upgrade path).
- **Chroma vs Qdrant vs Milvus vs pgvector** (general pattern from docs; no single strong citation): embedded file-backed store (Chroma) is fastest to prototype and matches "in-memory or file-backed" in the assignment; Qdrant/Milvus are server-grade; **pgvector wins when provenance metadata (pages, timestamps, scores) must be SQL-queryable** — a key architectural point since our reports need page/timestamp lookups alongside similarity.
- **Async queue architecture:** production plagiarism APIs themselves are async (Copyleaks: submit → webhook → fetch result; Quetext: submit async "report id", poll). This confirms the correct shape for our scaling brief: upload → Celery/RQ (or SQS) → worker pool with GPU/CPU split (ASR on GPU workers, embedding on CPU) → FAISS/Qdrant index → report generation queue. Copyleaks doc: [scanning webhook model](https://docs.copyleaks.com/mcp/scanning).
- **GPU vs CPU performance anchors:** all-MiniLM-L6-v2 ~15 ms/chunk on CPU (~5 ms GPU) ([NICxKMS comparison](https://github.com/NICxKMS/semantic-search-mcp/blob/main/docs/MODEL-COMPARISON.md)); faster-whisper tiny/base run comfortably faster-than-real-time on CPU int8 (~20× RT on x86 CPU; [benchmark article](https://www.promptquorum.com/power-local-llm/local-whisper-stt-comparison-2026)) so short MP4s need no GPU; GPU is justified only for hours-scale transcription or ≥100k-chunk embedding.
- **What evidence says:** FAISS IndexFlatIP (normalized) is both correct and sufficient in the prototype; the scaling brief can defensibly state (a) CPU-only for the batch of ≤10k docs (300–600k vectors ≈ 0.5 GB), (b) memory-opt disk index or IVFFlat/HNSW for ≥1M chunks, (c) async queue with per-file idempotent jobs, (d) metadata kept in SQL/JSON, vectors in the ANN index, (e) GPU pool only if ASR volume grows, matching ETS-scale deployments that contained cost by optimizing Whisper size choice rather than adding GPUs.

## 4. Design directives for our prototype

1. **Normalize all modalities to text, then run ONE similarity stack.** ETS' published 92%/87% accuracy pipeline (human transcript vs ASR-transcript) proves that `Whisper → transcript → text similarity` catches cross-modal reuse; do not build a separate audio-embedding path. Attach `{file, page, t0, t1}` metadata to every chunk at extraction time so every score is attributable to a location.
2. **Use faster-whisper tiny/base, int8, CPU** — ~4× faster than openai-whisper, real-time-grade on tiny/base, MIT-licensed, produces per-segment start/end timestamps; keep `vad_filter=True` to auto-drop silent stretches (this *is* the scenario-3 silent-MP4 detector: zero speech detected → flag file, don't crash).
3. **Embed with sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (384-d) as the requested default; document the e5/bge upgrade path.** Cap chunks at ≤200 tokens because MiniLM's context window is 256 tokens (any longer is silently truncated); chunk on sentence boundaries with 10–20% overlap (the playbook standard), producing 200–400-token chunks where the model allows.
4. **IndexFlatIP + faiss.normalize_L2 = exact cosine.** Use for ≤10k docs (≈600k chunks ≈ 0.5 GB RAM). For the 10k brief: switch point to IVFFlat with `nlist=4√N–16√N` or HNSW32 per official FAISS guidelines; quantified: 300k chunks ≈ 460 MB RAM ≪ RAM; 10k docs of 1500-word average ≈ 300–600k chunks.
5. **Meet Scenario 2 with *relative* thresholds, not absolute cosine.** Because all-MiniLM-L6-v2 paraphrase pairs commonly score 0.7–0.95 and unrelated pairs 0.2–0.5, set thresholds on a *corpus-relative* basis (e.g., a chunk pair counts if cosine > global mean + K·σ, or > 0.80 for the exact-semantic-similarity band specified by the assignment) and log the calibration — cite that Turnitin's own % doesn't have universal meaning and e5 warns 0.7–1.0 distribution.
6. **Boilerplate discount (Scenario 4) — winnowing's own approach:** fingerprint the shared case prompt / instruction questions / citations into a registry with special document IDs; every match whose nearest neighbor is a registry chunk is *reported separately* as "common-source overlap" rather than added to the plagiarism score; enforce minimum matched-chunk length (DrillBit's <14-words auto-exclusion but stricter: discounted if matched on < ~100 chars) and require ≥2 independent non-registry chunk matches before flagging HIGH.
7. **Self-match exclusion by document_id** (assignment explicitly required): build a chunk matrix; skip (i,j) where doc_id(i) == doc_id(j); also mirror Turnitin-style *small-source* exclusion (sources contributing <1.5% of the doc are listed but not counted in the headline percentage).
8. **Web-reference engine = 2-tier.** Tier 1 always-on: local reference KB (Wikipedia page sentences + case source text + a set of LLM-generated competitors) indexed with the same MiniLM model. Tier 2 optional: free search API (ddgs; Brave Search API 2k queries/mo free tier; Google CSE 100/day) sending distinctive sentence queries extracted via TF-IDF/noun-phrase (the classic PAN query formulation pattern), always with a graceful fallback to mock results when offline — mirroring Copyleaks' sandbox mode; rate-limit sleeps.
9. **AI detector = ensemble + long disclaimer.** Local classifier (verified HF IDs below) + GPT-2 perplexity + burstiness variance, all reported separately, thresholded conservatively, with an mid-band "uncertain" and an explicit anti-bias note referencing the Stanford 61% ESL false-positive study — mirror GPTZero's classification language (AI/Mixed/Human + confidence), Turnitin's withholding of 1–19% scores, and length gating (≥350 chars minimum like Copyleaks).
10. **Report format = Ouriginal/Copyleaks-style side-by-side plus location tokens:** JSON+MD for every file containing offending sentence, matched sentence, source file, page number (PDF) or start-end timestamp (MP4 from faster-whisper segments), chunk cosine, and a short natural-language explanation naming *the section* of overlap (not just a bare %) — mirroring Turnitin's per-source panel + Copyleaks' snippet viewer and HackerEarth's colored diff.
11. **Evaluation against a ground-truth manifest patterned on PAN + ETS:** generate `dataset_manifest.json` with per-pair labels (exact-copy pair, cross-modal pair, AI-generated doc, original pair, degraded file); compute precision/recall *per scenario family*, plus confusion matrix over HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW vs truth; report ≥2 honest failure modes (accented-speech transcription errors are the documented online failure [cf. ETS: ASR costs 5–10% accuracy]; ESL-text false positives on AI detection [cf. Stanford 61%]).
12. **Scaling brief structure (limn from the evidence):** (a) file → upload → Celery/RQ/SQS queue → extraction worker → whisper/GPU pool only if ASR hour-volume warrants; (b) chunk → embed (CPU pool, ~15 ms per chunk on MiniLM) → upsert vectors into FAISS(HNSW/IVF) or Qdrant; (c) metadata (pages, timestamps, scores) in Postgres/pgvector or plain SQLite/JSON — this mirrors how Copyleaks/Quetext's production APIs are actually built (async submit → webhook → fetch); (d) throughput estimate: 10k docs ≈ ≤500k chunks ≈ 3 GB RAM, ~4 hours per 1-GPU-ASR + 1-CPU-embed pipeline; (e) bottlenecks: ASR throughput (largest), embedding (CPU parallelizable), FAISS build (select proper index), report generation (I/O).

### Verified tech facts

- **sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2:** output dim **384**, 22.7M params, max seq **256 tokens** (per model card and [MODEL-COMPARISON doc](https://github.com/NICxKMS/semantic-search-mcp/blob/main/docs/MODEL-COMPARISON.md)); typical cosine of *unrelated* sentence pairs ≈ 0.2–0.5; paraphrase pairs commonly >0.75–0.9; use `model.similarity()` or normalize-L2 + dot product. (Same doc lists bge-small-en-v1.5 62.17 MTEB avg vs MiniLM 56.26.)
- **faiss-cpu pip package:** `pip install faiss-cpu numpy` ([FAISS wiki](https://github.com/facebookresearch/faiss/wiki/Building-from-source) and multiple tutorials use it). **`IndexFlatIP` + `faiss.normalize_L2(vecs)` = exact cosine similarity** (tutorial [code](https://buildwithaitoday.com/track/rag-knowledge/learn/rag-indexing_algorithms); reference [doc](https://github.com/endman100/skill-router-nexus/blob/main/AI-Research/faiss/faiss/references/index_types.md)). FAISS itself has no cosine metric switch.
- **faster-whisper** (`pip install faster-whisper`): CTranslate2 reimplementation of Whisper by SYSTRAN/Guillaume Klein, MIT, ~**4× faster than openai-whisper**, runs tiny/base at real-time-or-better on `compute_type="int8"` CPU; `segments` generator yields each segment with `.start`/`.end` timestamps (needed for the report). Whisper `tiny` (39M) WER ~7.6%, `base` (74M) ~5.0% English ([comparison](https://www.promptquorum.com/power-local-llm/local-whisper-stt-comparison-2026)).
- **Verified Hugging Face AI-detector model IDs** (all import via `transformers.pipeline("text-classification", model=...)`):
  - `openai-community/roberta-base-openai-detector` (aka `roberta-base-openai-detector`, `nbroad/openai-detector-base` mirror) — GPT-2-era detector, ~95% on GPT-2 output, **not** effective vs modern LLMs (modern ROC-AUC ≈ 0.31; see [third-party benchmark](https://huggingface.co/wasitaigeneratedcom/ai-text-detector-small)).
  - `roberta-large-openai-detector` — larger GPT-2 sibling, same caveat.
  - `Hello-SimpleAI/chatgpt-detector-roberta` — RoBERTa-base, HC3 (ChatGPT-vs-human) trained, ~500 MB, published as the standard ChatGPT open detector ([card](https://huggingface.co/Hello-SimpleAI/chatgpt-detector-roberta)).
  - `desklib/ai-text-detector-v1.01` — DeBERTa-v3-large, RAID leaderboard top at submission, ROC-AUC ≈ 0.875; 0.4B params (heavier but still CPU-runnable).
  - `wasitaigeneratedcom/ai-text-detector-small` ("tropa-mini") — DeBERTa-v3-small-ish, ROC-AUC 0.968, scans raw AI text with 93.2% recall at 0.5% FPR, **15.6% FPR on Liang' TOEFL ESL set** at that operating point — the most modern open-weights detector found, and explicitly transparent about its ESL bias.
  - *Not verified:* a model ID of the form `chaunguyen/gpt2-detector` — treat as folklore; use the verified ones above.
- **espeak-ng locally available:** confirmed on this machine at `/usr/bin/espeak-ng` (plus `/usr/bin/ffmpeg` at `/usr/bin/ffmpeg`), which covers both scripted TTS narration for the 10–30s MP4 test clips and video encoding for the demo media. Local Python is 3.14.8.
- **faiss-cpu AutoPara:** at 384 dims, ~600k vectors (≈10k typical submissions) is <1 GB of RAM — IndexFlatIP is exact and practical; IVFFlat/HNSW are needed only at ≥1M chunks ([FAISS guidelines wiki](https://github.com/facebookresearch/faiss/wiki/Guidelines-to-choose-an-index)).

**Research summary for downstream Spec Writer:** commercially, every serious plagiarism engine is text-only and web/database-indexed (Turnitin/iThenticate/Copyleaks/Ouriginal/DrillBit); video/audio spoken-content plagiarism has essentially only the ETS academic line and no shipped commercial product — our cross-modal top-layer (Whisper transcript → same text-similarity stack with timestamps) is therefore both novel and evidence-backed. AI-text detection is a graveyard of single signals (OpenAI classifier retired; hallucination-prone ensemble) whose published false-positive rate against non-native writers is 61% — mandate an ensemble + explicit "uncertain" band. Semantic+verbatim matching deep literature (PAN/CLEF) agrees on: two-stage retrieval (doc-level filter → chunk-level ANN), sentence-aware chunking 200–400 tokens with overlap, hybrid BM25+dense+cross-encoder rerank, thresholds calibrated per model not absolute. Boilerplate/common-source discount is a first-class published practice (winnowing's special-ID scheme; Turnitin's exclude-quotes/bibliography; DrillBit's <14-word auto-exclusion). FAISS's own guidance supports Flat for ≤10–30k docs and IVF/HNSW beyond, with L2-normalization + IP = cosine. Search-based external references = per-sentence keyphrase query to a free API, with a local Wikipedia/case-study KB as the always-on tier and mocks for CI. Verified tech facts table above is directly usable in the Spec.
